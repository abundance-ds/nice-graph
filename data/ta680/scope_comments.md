|Questions for<br>consultation|Celgene Ltd|Yes, lenalidomide maintenance treatment of newly diagnosed muliple myeloma<br>patients can be viewed as an innovative technology, with an<br>immunomodulatory mode of action.<br>Benefits of lenalidomide maintenance (in the context of clinically meaningful<br>improvements) can likely not be captured since QoL data on lenalidomide<br>maintenance was not collected in either IFM 2005-02 and CALGB 100104.<br>Therefore, only rudimentary QoL proxy data will be available, which might not<br>capture all treatment-specific QoL benefits.<br>Lenalidomide is an oral therapy and therefore can be self-administered. This<br>can be of immense help to patients who have mobility problems. However this<br>benefit is unlikely to be reflected in the standard QALY measure.<br>Data is available from 2 phase III trials - IFM 2005-02 and CALGB 100104 for<br>patients following stem cell transplant. In these trials, progression-free survival<br>(PFS) was used as primary endpoint, while response rates and overall survival,<br>OS, amongst others, were collected as secondary endpoints.|Comment noted.|

||NCRI/RCP/RCR<br>/ACP/JCCO|Yes, this technology is innovative, and represents a 'step-change' in the<br>mangement of this incurable cancer. This technology is able to double the time<br>to disease progression following stem cell transplantation, and as such is the<br>most significant innovation in the management of these patients since the<br>adoption of high dose therapy.  Results of the CALGB phase 3 study also<br>indicate a survival benefit. ||Myeloma UK|The use of maintenance treatment is widely regarded as having the potential to<br>make a substantial impact on the duration and quality of response (in particular<br>progression free survival) experienced by patients following autologous stem<br>cell transplantation.<br>The CALGB Phase III study has also recently reported that lenalidomide<br>maintenance post-autologous stem cell transplantation may also have a benefit<br>in improving survival.<br>There is currently an unmet need with regard to an approved, standard<br>maintenance strategy for myeloma patients in the UK.<br>The evidence suggests that lenalidomide is an effective maintenance treatment<br>in this patient population. As the first treatment to receive an anticipated licence<br>for maintenance, it offers a potential step-change in the management of<br>myeloma in the UK.<br>*Please see 'Comparators' section for a response to the 'Questions for<br>consultation' on the most appropriate comparators for lenalidomide<br>maintenance treatment.|Comment noted.|
