## **Comment 2: the draft scope** 

|**Section**|**Consultee/**<br>**Commentator**|**Comments [sic]**|Action|
|---|---|---|---|
|Background<br>information|Takeda UK|As noted, systemic anaplastic large cell lymphoma (sALCL) is a rare (ultra-<br>orphan) but aggressive type of non-Hodgkin T-cell lymphoma.<br>It should be noted that there are 2 subtypes of sALCL; Anaplastic lymphoma<br>kinase (ALK) positive (60%) and ALK negative (40%).<br>The outcomes for patients with ALK negative sALCL are significantly worse<br>than those with ALK positive disease and the treatment approach can also<br>differ. For patients with ALK negative disease, the treatment approach is<br>similar to peripheral T-cell lymphoma not otherwise specified (PTCL-NOS)<br>and may include a consolidative stem cell transplant during first remission.<br>Reference: Dearden et al., Guidelines for the Management of Mature T-cell and NK-cell<br>Neoplasms. British Committee for Standards in Haematology. Updated August 2013<br>For patients who relapse following front line therapy with a CHOP-based<br>regimen, the options are extremely limited. Of those treatments listed in the<br>draft scope only gemcitabine is available for use within the NHS. The other<br>medicines are either not licensed (at all) in Europe (pralatrexate and<br>romidepsin) or not licensed or funded for sALCL (bortezomib, alemtuzumab).|Comment noted.<br>The scope background<br>has been amended to<br>note the 2 subtypes of<br>systemic anaplastic<br>large cell lymphoma.<br>The scope background<br>has been amended to<br>remove the list of<br>treatment options after<br>CHOP-based<br>chemotherapy.|
