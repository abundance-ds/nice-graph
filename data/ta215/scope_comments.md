|Questions for<br>consultation|GlaxoSmithKline|Should immunotherapy be considered as an appropriate<br>comparator?<br>It is felt that immunotherapy (IFN alpha/IL-2) should not be<br>considered as a relevant comparator as it no longer constitutes a<br>standard treatment in the NHS. Indeed, UK oncologist estimated<br>that currently only around 5% of patients in the UK are receiving<br>immunotherapy as the first line treatment of advanced/metastatic<br>RCC and this population is diminishing rapidly. In addition, data<br>presented in 2005 by Negrier showed that the benefit of<br>immunotherapy was restricted to a very limited group of patients<br>and for the rest was a toxic and ineffective treatment.<br>It is worth noting that since no head to head data for pazopanib<br>versus sunitinib are currently available a indirect comparison via<br>IFN will be necessary for the puspose of our submission.<br>Furthermore, a randomised head to head trial comparing<br>pazopanib with sunitinib is currently being conducted. Preliminary<br>results from this study are expected to be available in Q2 2011.<br>How should best supportive care be defined?<br>(Zafar 2008). In the pazopanib pivotal study best supportive care<br>included transfusion of blood and blood products, treatment with<br>antibiotics, anti-emetics, anti-diarrhoeal agents, anti-hypertensive<br>agents, erythropoietin, or bisphosphonates, when appropriate.|Comment noted. It was<br>agreed that sunitinib is the<br>standard f care for most<br>people. However, for people<br>who are unsuitable<br>immunotherapy and best<br>supportive care are currently<br>the only available treatment<br>options.<br>Comment noted.|
