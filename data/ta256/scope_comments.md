|Appropriateness|Bayer plc|Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia1,2. The<br>prevalence of AF is increasing due to an aging population and increasing survival from<br>conditions predisposing to AF3.<br>The annual risk of stroke is five to six times greater in AF patients than in people with<br>normal heart rhythm4. A stroke in patients with AF is associated with greater mortality,<br>morbidity, and longer hospital stays than those without AF5. Strokes associated with<br>AF also tend to cause greater disability and reduced discharge to the patient‘s own<br>home6.<br>The impairment associated with stroke can have a substantial negative impact on<br>patients‘ quality of life.<br>Approximately 86% of the estimated prevalent population with AF are considered to<br>have a moderate to high risk of stroke and eligible for anticoagulation7.<br>The evidence however suggests that there is considerable under-use of<br>anticoagulants amongst patients with an established diagnosis of AF who are at high<br>risk of stroke8.<br>Warfarin, the main comparator, has a number of limitations, including:<br><br>A narrow therapeutic index with a fine balance between decreasing the risk of<br>thrombosis and increasing the risk of haemorrhage<br><br>The requirement for dose adjustment using frequent, inconvenient and costly INR<br>monitoring<br><br>Response influenced by diet, concomitant medications, herbal supplements,<br>intercurrent illness|Comment noted.<br>The background<br>section of the<br>scope has been<br>updated to reflect<br>this information.|
