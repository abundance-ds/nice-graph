|Comparators|GSK|In relation to the question of which chemotherapy regimens are most commonly used in current<br>clinical practice for the subsequent treatment of people who have relapsed after a complete or<br>partial response to first-line therapy, audit data, market research and clinician opinion suggest the<br>following:<br>Sixty percent of patients receive active treatment as their first line therapy for SCLC; 10% of<br>patients receive specialist palliative care and 24% are classed as receiving 'no specific anti cancer<br>treatment' (1). Approximately 85% of patients with extensive disease and more than 90% of those<br>with limited disease respond to initial therapy, but nearly all of these patients will relapse, usually<br>within a year (2). Approximately three quarters of patients that relapse receive best supportive care<br>(3, 4). Of the remaining 25% that do receive a second line chemotherapy, around 40% receive<br>CAV, 15% receive platinum plus etoposide, and 15% receive IV topotecan monotherapy (3).<br>Since 75% of patients who relapse do not receive active treatment, we strongly believe that best<br>supportive care should be considered as the key comparator in this appraisal.<br>CAV is the treatment of choice in the majority of patients who receive a second line chemotherapy,<br>and therefore should be considered as a relevant comparator in patients for whom IV<br>chemotherapy is appropriate.<br>Topotecan is indicated for the treatment of patients with relapsed SCLC for whom retreatment with<br>their first line regimen is not considered appropriate. Since platinum drugs and etoposide are first<br>line regimens, and their use in the second line setting tends to be as retreatments, we believe that<br>these regimens are not valid comparators.<br>IV topotecan is used in a minority of patients and as such we believe that it should not be<br>considered as a standard comparator.<br>As mentioned above, radiotherapy has an important role to play, but its use tends to be as part of<br>the total first line treatment protocol. Therefore we believe that radiotherapy is not a relevant<br>comparator in relapsed disease.<br>In summary, we believe that relevant comparators in this appraisal are:<br>- Best supportive care<br>- CAV<br>(1) The Information Centre for Health and Social Care.
