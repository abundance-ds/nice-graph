||Promega|For the subsection entitled “**Microsatellite Instability**”, consideration should<br>be given to further specifying highly sensitive markers for detecting MSI in<br>colorectal tumours.  Accurate identification of MSI-H patients is critical to the<br>effectiveness of pembrolizumab in this population.  A recent panel of experts<br>from the European Society of Medical Oncology (ESMO) developed a set of<br>recommendations for assessing mismatch repair status of solid tumours in<br>patients being considered for immunotherapy (1).  This expert panel<br>recognized that traditional molecular testing based on PCR amplification of<br>microsatellite markers is typically performed via one of two marker panels:<br>one using a mixture of mononucleotide and dinucleotide repeats (i.e., the<br>Bethesda panel BAT25, BAT26, D5S346, D2S123, D17S250), and the other<br>using a five poly-A mononucleotide repeats (BAT25, BAT26, NR21, NR24,<br>NR27).  It was noted the five poly-A panel is recommended given its higher<br>sensitivity and specificity.  Both panels have been and are being used to<br>assess MSI in immunotherapy clinical trials.  The poly-A panel was used in<br>the Keynote-016, -059 and in confirmatory testing in the Keynote-158 trials for<br>pembrolizumab (2-7).  The markers BAT25 and BAT26 are common to both<br>panels and have been well-established as highly sensitive for detecting<br>microsatellite instability in colorectal cancers as well as across genetically<br>diverse human populations due to their quasimonomorphic nature (8).<br>Promega would therefore recommend additional detail to be included in the<br>scope along the following lines: “MSI status is determined by PCR<br>(polymerase chain reaction)-based analysis of tissue samples from colorectal<br>cancer tumours to detect a standardised panel of DNA markers,**_including_**<br>**_BAT25 and BAT26._**”|Thank you for your<br>comment.
