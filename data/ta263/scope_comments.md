## **Response to consultee and commentator comments on the draft remit and draft scope** 

**Comment 1: the draft scope** 

|**Section**|**Consultees**|**Comments**|**Action**|
|---|---|---|---|
|Background<br>information|Breakthrough<br>Breast Cancer|The draft scope states that “there were over 45,000 women and around 300<br>men newly diagnosed with breast cancer in England and Wales during 2008.”<br>This figure apparently includes cases of ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS).  As<br>the usual figure cited for England and Wales1is “over 42,000” and does not<br>include DCIS  it should be made clear that this number includes cases of DCIS<br>to avoid confusion.<br>The sentence on the number of breast cancer patients who will eventually<br>develop metastatic breast cancer should be slightly changed to improve<br>accuracy: “…it is estimated that around 35% of those presenting with early or<br>localised breast cancer will develop metastatic breast cancer in the 10 years<br>following diagnosis. [Secondary Breast Cancer Taskforce (2007). Stand up and<br>be counted: the need for the collection of data on incidence of secondary<br>breast cancer and survival. Breast Cancer Care.]<br>1e.g. on the Cancer Research UK “Breast cancer: UK incidence statistics”<br>website athttp://info.cancerresearchuk.org/cancerstats/types/breast/incidence/|Comment noted. The scope<br>has been amended to state<br>that: “there were over 42,000<br>women and around 300 men<br>newly diagnosed with breast<br>cancer in England and Wales<br>during 2008.”<br>The sentence on the number<br>of breast cancer patients who<br>will eventually develop<br>metastatic breast cancer has<br>been changed to: “…it is<br>estimated that around 35% of<br>those presenting with early or<br>localised breast cancer will<br>develop metastatic breast<br>cancer in the 10 years<br>following diagnosis.”|
