The trabecular meshwork is<br>responsible for approximately 70-96% of aqueous humor outflow.3–5 For<br>primary open-angle glaucoma, the pathology underlying increased intraocular<br>pressure resides in the trabecular meshwork.6 Currently available treatments<br>for primary open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension do not act directly on<br>the trabecular meshwork to reduce intraocular pressure.<br> <br>Rho-kinase (ROCK) inhibitors are the first new class of drugs for glaucoma<br>since the mid-1990s, and their unique mechanism of action targeting the<br>trabecular meshwork represents an opportunity to evolve primary open-angle<br>glaucoma and ocular hypertension treatment.7,8<br>ROCK signalling has been identified as an important regulator of trabecular<br>meshwork outflow.9 The ROCK pathway is involved in cytoskeletal integrity of<br>cells, synthesis of extracellular matrix components for aqueous humor outflow<br>and in the permeability of endothelial cells with Schlemm’s canal.10,11<br>Inhibition of the ROCK signalling pathway has been associated with the<br>relaxation of the trabecular meshwork, with subsequent increase in aqueous<br>humor outflow facility and reduced episcleral venous pressure, which leads to<br>a reduction in intraocular pressure.12<br>Existing pre-clinical and clinical scientific evidence suggests that the ROCK<br>inhibitor netarsudil lowers intraocular pressure through at least two<br>mechanisms: by increasing trabecular outflow and by reducing episcleral<br>venous pressure.13–17|which the technology<br>may or may not be<br>innovative will be<br>considered by the<br>Technology Appraisal<br>Committee.
