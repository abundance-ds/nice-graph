||Ltd|Canagliflozin inhibits the selective sodium glucose-cotransporter 2<br>(SGLT2), blocking the reabsorption of filtered glucose from the tubular<br>lumen (back into the bloodstream) and lowering the renal threshold for<br>glucose, resulting in the excretion of excess glucose, thereby directly<br>lowering glucose concentrations in individuals with elevated glucose levels,<br>such as patients with T2DM. This mechanism does not depend upon the<br>action of insulin (ie, is insulin-independent).  In addition to lowering glucose<br>in hyperglycaemic patients, the urinary glucose excretion also results in: 1)<br>an osmotic diuresis leading to a reduction in systolic blood pressure and 2)<br>a loss of calories (4 kcalories per gram of carbohydrate, with typical<br>excretion of approximately 100 grams per day in patients with T2DM) and<br>therefore a reduction in body weight.<br>Canagliflozin is also a low potency inhibitor of the selective sodium<br>glucose-cotransporter 1 (SGLT1), a key gut glucose transport mechanism.<br>At the higher dose (300 mg), high concentrations in the gastrointestinal<br>lumen during drug dissolution prior to drug absorption are likely sufficient to<br>inhibit the SGLT1, thereby reducing the rate of glucose absorption from the<br>intestine, resulting in reduction of postprandial glucose excursions.<br>To the list of regimens studied, the following should be added:<br>For those on sulphonylurea monotherapy, canagliflozin is being studied<br>as a dual therapy in comparison with placebo.|opportunity to describe the<br>mode of action of cangliflozin<br>in detail in their submission.<br>The scope has been amended<br>to include the additional trial<br>regimen included in this<br>comment.|
