||Pfizer|As per the trial protocol for NCT01828112, pemetrexed and docetaxel are the<br>appropriate treatment options for patients as an alternative to LDK378 who<br>have previously been treated with platinum doublet cytotoxic chemotherapy<br>and crizotinib, therefore should be included as relevant comparators in this<br>appraisal. As described in the Appropriateness section, the inclusion criteria<br>(point #2) for this trial requires that patients receive previous treatment with<br>crizotinib and one regimen of platinum doublet cytotoxic chemotherapy,<br>therefore crizotinib is not a relevant comparator in this appraisal. This is in line<br>with current clinical practice, where crizotinib is routinely used in patients with<br>ALK positive NSCLC previously treated with chemotherapy.<br>As per the draft scope, best supportive care is also an appropriate comparator.<br>If NICE was to proceed with the appraisal of LDK378 for people with ALK-<br>positive NSCLC who have received prior chemotherapy only, Pfizer agree with<br>NICE’s decision to include drugs available through the Cancer Drugs Fund<br>(CDF) as relevant comparators in this technology appraisal, as crizotinib is<br>routinely used for the treatment of patients with ALK-positive NSCLC. Not<br>including these technologies would reflect an inaccurate representation of how<br>patients with previously treated ALK positive NSCLC are treated in England.|Comment noted. Although the<br>phase 3 trial compared<br>ceritinib with docetaxel or<br>pemetrexed in patients who<br>had received chemotherapy<br>and crizotinib, the clinician at<br>the scoping workshop<br>indicated that neither<br>docetaxel nor pemetrexed<br>would be considered to be in<br>routine use in the UK at that<br>stage in therapy, and that best<br>supportive care would be<br>offered instead. No action<br>required.|

|Economic<br>analysis|Pfizer|Pfizer believe this appraisal should evaluate the clinical and cost-effectiveness<br>of LDK378 in ALK-positive NSCLC patients following treatment with platinum<br>doublet cytotoxic chemotherapy and crizotinib.<br>If NICE proceed with the appraisal of LDK378 following chemotherapy only,<br>and therefore consider crizotinib as a comparator as it is currently available<br>through the CDF, Pfizer would like to request clarity on NICE’s new methods,<br>as it is unclear how exploring cost-effectiveness against a therapy that has<br>been deemed to be not cost-effective by the Institute will be integrated into the<br>decision making process.|Comment noted. Sections<br>6.2.1–4 of the Guide to the<br>methods of technology<br>appraisal 2013outline the<br>Committee’s approach to the<br>relevance and<br>appropriateness of<br>comparators. In particular,<br>section 6.2.3 states that when<br>the assessment suggests that<br>an established practice may<br>not be considered a good use<br>of NHS resources, the<br>Committee will decide whether<br>to include it as a comparator in<br>the appraisal after reviewing<br>an incremental cost–utility<br>analysis. No action required.|
|Equality and<br>Diversity|Pfizer|Pfizer note that the purpose of STAs, as described in the directions of the<br>Secretary of State for Health and captured in section 1.2.2 of NICE’s “Guide to<br>the methods of technology appraisal 2013”, is to appraise the health benefits<br>and the costs of those technologies referred by the Secretary of State for<br>Health and to make recommendations to the NHS in England and Wales. The<br>CDF provides additional funding to enable patients within NHS England to<br>access drugs that are not routinely funded by the NHS. As the CDF does not<br>provide funding for the treatment of patients in Wales, Pfizer would like to<br>request clarity on the impact this technology appraisal will have for patients in<br>Wales, as including technologies that are only available through the CDF will<br>not reflect the reality of patient treatment in Wales.|Comment noted. As part of the<br>Health and Social Care Act in<br>2012, NICE became a non-<br>departmental public body in<br>England only in April 2013.<br>Therefore, NICE issues<br>guidance for England only. No<br>action required.|
