|||Our improved understanding of the MAPK pathway and mechanisms of<br>resistance has both led to the rapid development (within 4 years) of the novel<br>MEK inhibitor, trametinib as well as its study in combination with dabrafenib.<br>The combination of dabrafenib and trametinib has substantially increased<br>PFS and OS, and increased RR by around 50%, with patients achieving at<br>least complete stabilisation of disease, relative to BRAF inhibitor<br>monotherapy efficacy.<br>The combination of dabrafenib and trametinib shows substantially less skin<br>toxicity than vemurafenib, although there are some unique toxicities<br>associated with combination therapy that have been shown to be<br>manageable.<br>The combination of dabrafenib and trametinib meets the end of life criteria by<br>offering an extension to life of at least three additional months versus<br>vemurafenib and dabrafenib – the current standards of care. The patient<br>population with BRAF V600 mutation positive melanoma is small and has a<br>median life expectancy of less than 24 months where there is no alternative<br>treatment of comparable benefit. The assessment of this medicine under<br>these criteria makes allowances for some of the benefit that may not be<br>captured in the QALY for patients with a severe disease and a short life<br>expectancy.||
|Other<br>considerations|British<br>Association of<br>Dermatologists|It is well recognised that combination treatment leads to less side effects<br>especially cutaneous toxicity and therefore less need for other specialist<br>healthcare input and investigations such as biopsy extra tests etc. It perhaps<br>is difficult to establish exactly the cost saving on this but it should be taken<br>note of.|Comment noted.
