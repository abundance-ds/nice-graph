||Celgene|MDS patients currently have few approved therapeutic options.<br>Erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs) work on early stage of erythroid<br>progenitor cells and, although often prescribed in MDS, have limited effect.<br>In many patients with lower-risk MDS, anaemia will eventually become<br>resistant to ESAs and will require repeated RBC transfusions. There is also<br>a cohort of Low Risk MDS patients who are ineligible/ intolerant to ESAs.<br>Long-term RBC transfusion dependence has several detrimental clinical<br>effects including iron overload, and a negative impact on patients’ quality of<br>life (QoL).<br>`-`<br>Luspatercept works by targeting specific TGF-beta proteins involved in late-<br>stage RBC maturation, stimulating RBC differentiation and thereby<br>preventing anaemia. This mechanism of action has the potential to be<br>transformative for patients with serious RBC disorders by significantly<br>reducing or eliminating the need for frequent and lifelong blood transfusions.|Comment noted. The<br>company will have an<br>opportunity to provide<br>evidence on the<br>innovative nature of its<br>product in its<br>submission. No action<br>required.|
|Questions for<br>consultation|Celgene|There are no licensed treatment options for adult patients with very low, low<br>or intermediate risk MDS who have associated anaemia except RBC<br>transfusions.<br>Best supportive care (BSC) is outlined appropriately and iron chelation may<br>also be considered as part of BSC. Other supportive treatment, such as<br>platelets, GCSF and antibiotics may be used due to the complications<br>associated with the disease.|Comments noted. No<br>action required.|
