||Schering-Plough|By limiting itself to only consider new indications, the new guidance will omit<br>new evidence collected for existing indications since the publication of TA75<br>and TA106. The most important evidence collected by Schering-Plough<br>comes from the IDEAL trial (Study ID P03471, completed 2008) which<br>showed that patients with genotype 1 HCV respond differently to treatment<br>depending on whether they receive PEG-IFN α-2a plus RBN or PEG-IFN α-<br>2b plus RBN. Namely, while the sustained virological response rates were<br>similar for both therapies, patients receiving PEG-IFN α-2b plus RBN<br>demonstrated better predictability at week 12 and were less likely to relapse<br>after an end-of-treatment response than patients receiving PEG-IFN α-2a<br>plus RBN.<br>Were the existing NICE guidance on pegylated interferons overhauled, we<br>would not expect that data from the IDEAL trial would materially alter<br>existing NICE guidance, i.e. it would not lead to the rejection of drugs<br>previously recommended. However, the data could lead to differential<br>budget impact estimates for PEG-IFN α-2a & RBN and PEG-IFN α-2b &<br>RBN and consequently different cost-effectiveness estimates.|Comment noted. Since the<br>IDEAL trial is not expected to<br>materially alter existing NICE<br>guidance, the focus of this<br>appraisal will be the licence<br>extensions to the<br>peginterferons.|
