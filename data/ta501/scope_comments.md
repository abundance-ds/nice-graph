||Breakthrough<br>Breast Cancer|Patients receiving radiotherapy for primary breast cancer are<br>required to attend hospital five times a week for three to five<br>weeks. This is very disruptive to a patient’s life and means that<br>a return to normalcy after breast cancer treatment is delayed. In<br>addition, attending hospital daily can be expensive for some<br>patients as they may incur costs of petrol or public transport and<br>parking costs. For patients who live far away from the hospital,<br>they may have to arrange to stay near the hospital while they<br>are having radiotherapy treatment. This causes further<br>disruption and means that patients may be away from their<br>homes and families for extended periods of time and may need<br>to make arrangements regarding childcare or caring for other<br>relatives.<br>Prompt implementation of this technology may mean that<br>patients will be able to avoid attending hospital on a daily basis<br>and resume their normal lives much faster. Implementation of<br>this technology may also be beneficial to older breast cancer<br>patients who may in some cases be frail and find travelling to<br>hospital every day is very difficult. This is important as breast<br>cancer is more common among older women with more than<br>80% of breast cancers being diagnosed in women over the age<br>of 50 (1).<br>Breast Cancer: UK Incidence Statistics – Age (2009), Cancer<br>Research UK,http://www.cancerresearchuk.org/cancer-<br>info/cancerstats/types/breast/incidence/#age|Comment noted.|
