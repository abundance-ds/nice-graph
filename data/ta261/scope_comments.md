||Bayer|DVT has an annual incidence of between 48 and 182 per 100,000. The<br>figure often quoted is 1 per 1000 (9).<br>Assuming a UK population of 61 million (10), the annual incidence of DVT<br>could fall between approximately 29,000 and 111,000. Alternatively, using<br>the figure of 1 per 1000 and a population of 61 million, gives an annual<br>incidence of 61,000.<br>The clinical course of DVT might be complicated by PE, recurrent<br>episodes of DVT and the development of post-thrombotic syndrome (PTS)<br>as well as Chronic Thromboembolic Pulmonary Hypertension (CTEPH).<br>Initial management of DVT is with SC LMWH or IV or SC heparin.<br>Warfarin is started at the same time and heparin continued for at least 5<br>days and until the INR has been in range for 2 consecutive days. Course<br>length depends on clinical aspects of the case.<br>As many patients are managed as outpatients, administration of LMWH<br>may require district nurse support and/or time to train the patient or carer<br>to self-inject. Warfarin has a number of limitations including a narrow<br>therapeutic index, response influenced by diet and drug interactions and<br>the requirement for dose adjustment and frequent INR monitoring.<br>There is no need for routine monitoring of coagulation parameters with<br>rivaroxaban. Introduction of rivaroxaban is therefore likely to result in a<br>reduced demand on costly anticoagulant services. Rivaroxaban also offers<br>a single drug approach which will simplify management of DVT. There are<br>no additional monitoring costs associated with rivaroxaban.|Comments noted. Following<br>the scoping workshop an<br>appraisal of rivaroxaban was<br>considered appropriate.|
