|Background<br>information|Movetis|A step up approach to the treatment of chronic constipation is employed by<br>clinicians in the UK. The first step in treatment is likely to be through the use of<br>diet and lifestyle modifications. As symptoms progress, laxatives of various<br>kinds will be introduced but will provide diminished effectiveness over time. The<br>most difficult problem relates to patients who have been suffering for a<br>significant length of time and have exhausted the available therapeutic options.<br>Only these patients who are considered to have ‘failed’ on laxatives will be<br>considered for treatment with prucalopride. As such the NICE evaluation must<br>acknowledge that prucalopride is not just another laxative but represents a new<br>and innovative technology that is entirely targeted upon patients who have<br>been suffering significant symptoms and quality of life deficits for an extended<br>period of time.|Comment noted. |Comparators|Movetis|Determining the ‘gold standard’ treatment currently provided by the NHS to the<br>target population for prucalopride is difficult given that by definition such<br>patients are laxative refractory. However, some form of clinical support will be<br>provided to patients either in the form of switching, combining and/or increasing<br>the dose of different laxatives (with limited efficacy) or rectal interventions such<br>as manual evacuation, suppositories and enemas to provide short term relief.<br>Given the limited efficacy of laxatives in the target population for prucalopride<br>the comparator should incorporate the frequency and effectiveness of non-<br>laxative interventions (rectal interventions and bowel surgery). The NICE scope<br>specifically emphasises that when oral laxative therapy is ineffective, a<br>suppository or enema may be appropriate. As such it is important for the NICE<br>evaluation to acknowledge that in this target population laxative therapy is of<br>unproven benefit.<br>This lack of proven efficacy underpins the assumption made in the clinical trials<br>that in this target population the  efficacy of laxatives can be appropriately<br>equated with the placebo response in the clinical trial. Such an approach<br>equating placebo response as a proxy for standard care is justifiable when an<br>entirely new therapeutic option becomes available for treatment provision in a<br>therapeutic area where no effective therapy currently exists. This is the case<br>with prucalopride where a new and effective treatment is being made available<br>to patients with no existing effective therapy. In the absence of such effective<br>treatment patients end up as ‘revolving door’ patients, frequently seeking<br>advice from GPs and specialists who are frustrated by the limited therapeutic<br>options available to them.<br>The scope must therefore emphasize the innovative nature of prucalopride as<br>a first in class highly selective entero-kinetic 5-HT4 receptor agonist. Equally<br>the exceptional nature of the target population and the extremely limited<br>therapeutic options currently available to treat this patient group needs to be<br>emphasised in the NICE scoping framework.|Comment noted. The scope<br>has been amended to include<br>invasive procedures such as<br>direct rectal interventions and<br>bowel surgery as comparator<br>technologies.<br>Comment noted.<br>Comment noted. The<br>description of prucalopride in<br>the scope has been updated.<br>The target population has<br>been amended to reflect the<br>licensed indication|

|Outcomes|Movetis|The NICE review should acknowledge the breadth and depth of the clinical<br>evidence generated in support of the efficacy of prucalopride in its’ target<br>population. The clinical trial programme specifically incorporated both 'physical'<br>outcome measures (number of Spontaneous and Complete Bowel Movements<br>(SCBM)) and patient reported outcomes measures (Patient Assessment of<br>Constipation Quality of Life Questionnaire (PAC-QOL), Patient Assessment of<br>Constipation Symptoms Questionnaire (PAC-SYM) and patient satisfaction).<br>This variety of outcome measures was specifically utilised in an attempt to<br>ascertain which single or range of outcome measures best capture the<br>therapeutic gain to the target patient population for prucalopride. In this regard<br>Movetis have a broad spectrum of evidence to support the clinical<br>effectiveness of prucalopride irrespective of whether the analysis is undertaken<br>for physical outcomes (number of SCBMs) or patient reported outcomes (PAC-<br>QOL, PAC-SYM and patient satisfaction). Prucalopride therefore has a very<br>strong evidence base utilising a wide range of outcome measures.<br>The ‘softer’ but patient focussed outcome measures have been extensively<br>validated and shown to be an effective means of capturing the patient<br>perspective with regard to the outcomes from treatment that are of greatest<br>value to patients. Thus the outcome measures used in the prucalopride trials<br>contain a combination of ‘outcome’ measures that effectively capture the full<br>benefits experienced by the patient population as a consequence of successful<br>treatment in this therapeutic area. In addition in recognition of the importance<br>of long term evidence in supporting claims of treatment effectiveness in chronic<br>populations a 52 week analysis has been undertaken evaluating patient<br>satisfaction with treatment. The comprehensive evidence base therefore<br>provides strong evidence of the physical benefits arising from prucalopride in<br>the short to medium term supported by evidence of its’ impact upon patient<br>quality of life and satisfaction over the longer term.|Comment noted.|

|Economic<br>analysis|Movetis|The development of a high quality economic model focussed on UK costs and<br>patterns of care represents the essential bedrock of the NICE submission<br>developed by Movetis. The QALYs estimates derived are supported by other<br>approaches over and above the use of QALYs which may have greater<br>relevance in assessing outcomes in chronic constipation.  However the<br>economic evaluation undertaken shows that the QALY framework is an<br>appropriate method for capturing outcomes generated by Prucalopride in this<br>therapeutic area. Preliminary analysis of the utility data generated from the<br>mapping exercise between disease specific and generic utility data undertaken<br>by Movetis has estimated an average utility gain of 0.04 QALYs for patients<br>treated with Prucalopride. This translates into a cost per QALY which<br>approximates to £18,000 - a figure which is within the guidelines for<br>acceptance normally utilised by NICE. In addition our estimates of utility are<br>generated using highly conservative assumptions and thus any changes to<br>such assumptions would be likely to enhance the cost effectiveness ratios<br>estimated for Prucalopride.<br>In addition to the QALY analysis the economic model can generate a range of<br>additional cost-effectiveness parameters such as the cost per patient achieving<br>normality (3 SCBMs)), the cost per additional SCBM, or the cost per patient<br>achieving satisfaction with therapy. In general we can present the economic<br>data in whichever way is required by NICE.|Comment noted. The clinical<br>and cost effectiveness of<br>prucalopride will be evaluated<br>based on available evidence<br>for its licensed indication.|
