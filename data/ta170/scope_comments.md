## **Rivaroxaban for the prevention of venous thromboembolism after elective orthopaedic surgery of the lower limbs** 

## **Comment 1: the draft remit**

|Appropriateness|Bayer|Each year there are over 25,000 deaths due to venous thromboembolism (VTE) in<br>England (1).Current guidelines recommend that in addition to mechanical prophylaxis,<br>patients at increased risk of VTE and those undergoing orthopaedic surgery should be<br>offered low molecular weight heparin (LMWH) or fondaparinux, and patients<br>undergoing hip fracture surgery or hip replacement with one or more risk factors for<br>VTE should have their LMWH or fondaparinux therapy continued for 4 weeks after<br>surgery (2).  Rivaroxaban is an oral, once-daily direct Factor Xa inhibitor.  Phase III<br>trials have shown that rivaroxaban significantly reduces the risk of VTE in patients<br>undergoing total knee replacement surgery and total hip replacement surgery<br>compared with enoxaparin (3-5).  As an effective and convenient, once-daily oral<br>treatment rivaroxaban offers a convenient treatment option that would aid the<br>implementation of the current NICE guidelines as it is anticipated that the oral route of<br>administration for rivaroxaban will be more acceptable than currently available<br>subcutaneous injections of LMWH or fondaparinux.<br>(1) House of Commons Select Committee 2005.  The prevention of VTE in<br>hospitalised patients. |Background<br>information|Bayer|The current NICE guidelines on venous thromboembolism in patients<br>undergoing surgery make specific recommendations for patients undergoing<br>orthopaedic surgery.  These recommend that patients having elective<br>orthopaedic surgery should be offered mechanical prophylaxis and either<br>LMWH or fondaparinux.
