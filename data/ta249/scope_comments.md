||Boehringer<br>Ingelheim|It is important that the definition of "moderate" risk and "high" risk of stroke is<br>clarified. The current NICE Clinical Guideline (36) provides for both warfarin<br>and aspirin as treatment options for patients at "moderate" risk. Therefore the<br>scope is incorrect to state that warfarin is the comparator in high risk patients<br>only with aspirin the comparator in moderate risk patients. Warfarin is<br>applicable for both.<br>The guideline is currently being reviewed, with publication planned for June<br>2010. Should the revised guideline advocate another risk stratification method<br>(such as CHADS2) then the "moderate" risk stratification may effectively<br>disappear and aspirin may no longer be a receommended as a standard<br>treatment option for such patients by the time of this appraisal. It is recognised<br>however that aspirin may be used in patients who are unsuitable for warfarin.<br>Aspirin is the only antiplatelet therapy licensed in this indication, other<br>antiplatelet therapies (e.g. clopidogrel or dipyridamole) are not licensed in this<br>indication.<br>The "best alternative care" for patients at moderate to high risk of stroke is<br>adjusted-dose warfarin and this should be the principle comparator in this<br>appraisal.
