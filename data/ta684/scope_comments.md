|||***************** **** *********************** ************************** ******<br>*************************** ***********<br>Nivolumab will be the first immunotherapy agent to receive a MA for the<br>above indication from the EMA. It specifically binds to PD-1 receptor on the<br>surface of immune cells and restores T-cell activity by blocking the binding of<br>the PD-L1 and PD-L2 ligands found at the tumour site to PD-1 receptors on<br>immune cells. This approach, enabling the body’s own immune system to<br>target cancer, is novel and is viewed by physicians and patient groups as a<br>‘step-change’ in its management.<br>Patients with resected stage III or IV melanoma have an increased risk of<br>recurrent disease which is associated with treatment challenges and poor<br>survival outcomes of advanced and metastatic disease and increased health<br>care costs.7Nivolumab acts primarily within the tumour microenvironment,<br>while adjuvant therapy targets micrometastatic disease which is the source of<br>future mortality from melanoma recurrence, therefore reducing the risk of<br>relapse in this population.8<br>In the CheckMate 238 clinical study, nivolumab administered as adjuvant<br>treatment was associated with statistically significant improvements in<br>recurrence-free survival (RFS) and distant metastasis-free survival (DMFS)<br>compared to ipilimumab6, which has previously demonstrated statistically<br>significant improvements in RFS and overall survival (OS) against placebo,<br>representative of routine surveillance.9Such prevention of disease recurrence<br>is associated with survival benefits, quality of life benefits and reduced<br>healthcare costs.||
