|Population|Diabetes UK|Given that the NICE Public Health Guidance 46 suggest the use of lower BMI<br>cut threshold for certain ethnic minority groups, it would be useful to reword<br>the population to reflect this. We suggest the population to be<br>‘Adults who have a BMI of<br><br>>30kg/m2 (obese)<br><br>>27kg/m2 to <30kg/m2 (overweight) in the presence of one or more<br>weight-related co-morbidities, or from Black, Asian or other ethnic<br>minority group’||
|Comparators|GSK|Since orlistat is the only authorised medicine for obesity treatment in the UK,<br>it is an appropriate comparator. However, it is recommended that only the<br>prescription dose, 120mg orlistat, be used for the following reasons:<br>•<br>Only the 120mg prescription dose is approved for long term use;<br>60mg orlistat  is only approved for short term use<br>•<br>The indication for orlistat 60mg is limited to weight loss whereas  the<br>120 mg dose is indicated for the treatment of obese patients, which includes<br>weight loss, maintenance and improvement in weight-related risk factors<br>•<br>60mg orlistat is over the counter and although it provides 80-85% of<br>the efficacy of the 120mg dose, if orlistat is being used as a comparator to a<br>prescription drug, the higher dose is a more appropriate option.|Comment noted. We<br>agree that 120mg of<br>orlistat is the most<br>appropriate dose as a<br>comparator.|
||RCP|The comparators seem reasonable but there is no direct head to head<br>comparison with orlistat. Most of the trials with orlistat were conducted over<br>20 years ago, so caution should be exercised when making indirect<br>comparisons; this is particularly true for conditions such as diabetes where<br>background standard therapy (for glucose and lipids especially) may be very<br>different now.||
