||Janssen|Ustekinumab offers a new mechanism of action in the treatment of UC.<br>Among the different cytokines implicated in the pathogenesis of UC, IL-12<br>and IL-23 are known to be responsible for T cell activation processes and<br>considered a key therapeutic target. Ustekinumab is expected to be the first<br>IL12/IL-23 inhibitor licensed for the treatment of UC.<br>Ustekinumab has been studied in the broadest UC trial population to date: the<br>UNIFI study includes patients who have failed conventional therapies, and<br>patients who have failed TNF-alpha inhibitors and/or vedolizumab.<br>Ustekinumab represents an innovation in the management of UC as it offers<br>a rapid onset of high remission and response rates in induction and<br>importantly this efficacy is maintained over the long term.  In addition,<br>ustekinumab is the only treatment to demonstrate combined endoscopic and<br>histological healing.<br>Ustekinumab has a convenient dosing schedule with IV induction and SC<br>dosing in maintenance (every 12 weeks), thereby reducing the treatment<br>burden for patients.<br>UC is associated with a significant burden and various indirect costs related<br>to loss of productivity.  These measures are not currently captured in the<br>QALY calculation and therefore the full benefit of ustekinumab in inducing<br>and maintaining remission and response cannot be fully captured.|Thank you for your<br>comment. If the topic<br>proceeds, the appraisal<br>committee will consider<br>any innovative aspects<br>of ustekinumab or<br>whether there are any<br>benefits that are not<br>adequately captured by<br>the QALY estimate.
