|||Anaemia-related fatigue is one of the main patient-reported symptoms of<br>PNH and evidence of improvements in fatigue are available from the<br>iptacopan clinical trials. However, standard generic health-related quality of<br>life (HRQoL) instruments utilised for generating health state utility values for<br>economic modelling may not be able to capture the full impact of fatigue on<br>patients’ HRQoL. In particular, the EQ-5D has been shown to have low<br>sensitivity to the impact of fatigue on HRQoL (6, 7). Consequently, benefits of<br>iptacopan in terms of reducing fatigue may not be fully captured in QALYs<br>based on health state utility values generated from a generic HRQoL<br>instrument.<br>Iptacopan is expected to be the first oral monotherapy for the treatment of<br>PNH. Benefits in terms of convenience of oral administration vs current IV/SC<br>infusions may not be fully captured in QALYs. Patients may perceive oral<br>administration as less burdensome and disruptive to their lives, as they would<br>not need to schedule regular IV infusions as with eculizumab or ravulizumab.<br>While pegcetacoplan can be self-administered, some patients may find twice<br>weekly SC infusions with a duration of 30 to 60 minutes each time-consuming<br>and cumbersome, and the requirement to store pegcetacoplan in the fridge<br>can, for example, impact patients’ travel plans. Some patients may also<br>perceive an oral treatment as less invasive compared with IV/SC infusion<br>treatments. These potential benefits may not be captured in QALYs.<br>An effective oral treatment may also have benefits on workplace productivity<br>that are not captured due to the healthcare system perspective of the<br>analysis. Time off work could be required for infusion of IV and SC<br>treatments. Anaemia-related fatigue and the requirement for blood<br>transfusions can also impact patients’ productivity.||
