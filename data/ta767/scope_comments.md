||MS Trust|The background information states that the relapsing form of MS is<br>characterised by periods of remission when symptoms are mild or disappear<br>altogether. Most people with MS experience one or more symptoms<br>continuously, but between relapses this background level will remain more or<br>less stable.<br>Background information does not capture the impact of MS on work and<br>family life.  People with MS are commonly diagnosed between the ages of 20<br>and 40 and may live with MS for 30-40 years.  The variable nature of MS<br>means that people given a diagnosis of MS and their families face many<br>years of uncertainty.  The disease can have a significant impact on work and<br>family life, both for the individual and for informal carers.<br>Background information does not capture the importance of early initiation of<br>disease modifying treatment. There is a considerable body of evidence and<br>medical consensus that starting treatment as soon as possible after diagnosis<br>leads to better outcomes.|Comment noted.
