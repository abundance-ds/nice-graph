|Innovation|Roche Products<br>Ltd|Yes. Faricimab is the first bispecific antibody designed for intravitreal injection<br>(IVT) that neutralises two distinct pathways of retinal disease: Ang-2 and<br>VEGF-A. Faricimab has been developed using CrossMab (monoclonal<br>antibody) technology. It independently binds and neutralises both Ang-2 and<br>VEGF-A with high specificity and potency without steric hindrance. The Fc<br>portion of the antibody has been specifically engineered for intraocular use to<br>reduce systemic exposure and inflammatory potential10.<br>Ang-2 has been shown to play a role in maintenance of the blood-retinal<br>barrier via its effect on pericyte survival and endothelial cell integrity. Pre-<br>clinical evidence suggests that dual inhibition of VEGF-A and Ang-2 can have<br>synergistic benefits including reducing leakage and microvascular<br>inflammation11-13. Faricimab may therefore lead to improved vascular<br>stabilisation and retinal function in patients with nAMD.<br>Studies TENAYA and LUCERNE are identical Phase III, multi-centre,<br>randomized, active comparator controlled, double-masked, parallel-group,<br>112-week studies, evaluating the efficacy, safety, durability, and<br>pharmacokinetics of the 6 mg dose of faricimab administered at up to 16-<br>week intervals compared with aflibercept monotherapy every 8 weeks (Q8W)<br>in patients with nAMD.
