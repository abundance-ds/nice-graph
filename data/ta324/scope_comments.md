|Background<br>information|Heart Rhythm<br>UK|The stated objective of this review is to appraise the clinical and cost<br>effectiveness of dual-chamber pacemakers for treating symptomatic<br>bradycardia in two groups:<br>1)**_people with sick sinus syndrome in whom there is no evidence of_**<br>**_impaired atrioventricular conduction_**– this is an appropriate objective.<br>2)**_people with atrioventricular block and continuous atrial fibrillation_**–<br>people in permanent (continuous) atrial fibrillation cannot benefit from dual<br>chamber pacing as their atria are electrically and mechanically dysfunctional.<br>The only appropriate pacing mode for symptomatic bradycardia and/or high<br>degree atrioventricular heart block, is single chamber ventricular rate<br>responsive pacing – VVIR or cardiac resynchronisation therapy (CRT). There<br>are no data to support dual chamber pacing in permanent atrial fibrillation and<br>this is not performed in clinical practice. There does not seem to be any<br>advantage to include this group in the appraisal.<br>Atrial fibrillation is classified as:<br><br>**_permanent_**when it is accepted and no attempt is made to regain sinus<br>rhythm.<br><br>It is classified as**_persistent_**when it is continuous for more than 7 days<br>and/or requires medical intervention (cardioversion) to terminate.<br><br>**_Paroxysmal_**atrial fibrillation is self-terminating, usually within 48-hours.<br><br>“**_Continuous_**” atrial fibrillation is not a recognised classification of the<br>arrhythmia and should be avoided for clarity.|Comments noted. The scope<br>will now only include those<br>with sick sinus syndrome and<br>no evidence of impaired<br>atrioventricular conduction.<br>The background section has<br>also been updated.|
