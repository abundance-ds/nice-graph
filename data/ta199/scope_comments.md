|Other<br>considerations|British<br>Association of<br>Dermatologists|The important matter which ought now to be considered explicitly is how to<br>incorporate joint AND skin disease into a composite evaluation of<br>“incremental cost per quality-adjusted life year”. This has not been achieved<br>as yet in NICE Guidance and some thought needs to be given as to how it<br>could be.  Patients who may just fail the hurdle for eligibility when assessed<br>by either joint or skin disease alone but who may well have more quality of life<br>impairment than many patients with predominant skin or joint disease.   This<br>is particularly true when PASI is used for assessment of the skin as this<br>underestimates the psychosocial and functional disability from acral, facial or<br>flexural disease. It may not be easy to come up with a satisfactory<br>formula. Nevertheless we shall be failing patients if we do not address this<br>matter.|Comment noted. Effect on<br>concomitant skin condition is<br>included as an outcome and<br>has been added as a<br>subgroup.|

||British Society for<br>Rheumatology|There is no evidence that these drugs are any more clinically effective in<br>certain subgroups than others, but they do seem to be effective across the<br>clinical spectrum of disease, e.g. enthesitis, spondyloarthritis, peripheral<br>arthritis.
