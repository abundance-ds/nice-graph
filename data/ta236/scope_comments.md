||Sanofi Aventis /<br>BMS|The study population for PLATO excludes patients who have had previous<br>revascularisation. This ACS population is at high risk of further events and in<br>the UK, about 26% of patients presenting with ACS will have had a previous<br>MI(MINAP 2006 report). The ESC EuroHeart survey reported that at 30 days,<br>about 16% of patients (n=828) discharged after an ACS episode had to be<br>readmitted with the majority being cardiac related, needing coronary<br>angiography, PCI, CABG or other cardiac surgery(Euro Heart survey<br>investigators Eur Heart J2006 27:2285-93). Therefore, the PLATO study is not<br>looking at a real world population where this drug may be used in patients with<br>previous resvacularisation. The risk benefit ratio has not been studied in that<br>particular population. It is also important to note that in the CURE study,<br>patients with a history of revascularisation(n=2246) had a 6% actual risk<br>reduction in the primary endpoint compared to 2.1% in the overall<br>group(n=12562)(CURE investigators, NEJM 2001 345:494-502) .<br>In the phase 2 study of AZD6140, DISPERSE-2(Cannon et al JACC 50:1844-<br>51 2007), a specific side effect of dyspnoea was noted, in about 10% of<br>patients at the lower dose(90mg BD) and 16% in the higher dose(180 mg BD).<br>In the context of ACS, this can obviously be a worrying symptom that could<br>lead to unnecessary escalation of treatment. This SAE will be monitored<br>closely in PLATO and it will be important to see if this side effect manifests<br>itself significantly in the larger phase 3 program and more importantly, if it leads<br>to discontinuation of therapy.|Comment noted. Following<br>consultation on the scope the<br>population has been amended<br>to state ‘Patients presenting<br>with ACS  irrespective of<br>whether they have undergone<br>revascularisation’.|
