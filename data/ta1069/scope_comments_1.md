|||Efgartigimod is not expected to be used to treat the emergency situation of<br>myasthenic crisis (MGFA class V)<br>Innovative potential<br>There are currently no NICE recommended products for gMG. As previously<br>described, current treatment options are aimed at symptom control, generally<br>take a significant time to work and are associated with long-term side-effects<br>and significant tolerability issues. Despite current therapies, many patients<br>with gMG continue to be affected by substantial disease burden, and<br>experience symptoms and morbidities that negatively impact their quality of<br>life. Approximately 90% of patients with gMG are unable to maintain normal<br>muscle strength without medication.1,2Furthermore, 10% to 15% of patients<br>are refractory to available treatments.3,4There is a significant unmet need for<br>effective targeted therapies for people with gMG.<br>Efgartigimod is a first in class antibody fragment specifically developed to<br>target the underlying mechanism of gMG by blocking FcRn (a protein in cells<br>which helps prevent IgG breakdown). By binding to FcRn at the same site as<br>endogenous IgG and thereby blocking its action, efgartigimod allows IgGs<br>(including the pathogenic autoantibodies causing the disruption to normal<br>nerve and muscle function) to be broken down and more quickly removed<br>from the body. It is a reduced size compared to full-length antibodies to<br>facilitate smaller dosing volumes and increased tissue penetration.<br>Efgartigimod therefore represents an innovative and targeted approach to<br>addressing the unmet need in gMG and if licensed and recommended for use<br>could offer the NHS a step change in treatment options for gMG.<br>Health-related benefits|Comment noted. No<br>action needed.|
