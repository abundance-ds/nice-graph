## **National Institute for Health and Clinical Excellence** 

## **Dronedarone for the treatment of atrial fibrillation and atrial flutter** 

**Comment 1: the draft remit** 

|**Section**|**Consultees**|**Comments**|**Action**|
|---|---|---|---|
|Appropriateness|Sanofi-aventis|Sanofi-aventis believe that an appraisal of dronedarone for the reduction of<br>cardiovascular (CV) hospitalisation or mortality in patients with a history of, or current,<br>atrial fibrillation (AF) and atrial flutter (AFL) is appropriate. Dronedarone has been<br>subject to an extensive clinical trial programme which has consistently demonstrated<br>the efficacy and safety of the product in the AF/AFL population (total number of<br>patients = 6285)<br>It is the first anti-arrhythmic product that has demonstrated a significant improvement<br>in reducing the risk of CV hospitalisation and mortality in patients with a history of or<br>current AF/AFL.|Comment noted|

|Wording|Sanofi-aventis|The pharmacological management of AF/AFL has been constrained by the lack of<br>newer, more effective technologies in recent years.  Those agents that are commonly<br>used have supporting data that has typically measured markers of disease such as<br>time to AF recurrence from sinus rhythm, but have never demonstrated a direct benefit<br>on key clinical outcomes such as mortality and morbidity. For the first time in the arena<br>of this significant disease a new anti-arrhythmic drug is able to offer evidence of<br>reduction of cardiovascular hospitalisation and mortality. The landmark ATHENA trial,<br>presented at the Heart Rhythm Symposium in May 2008 (www.hrsonline.org), showed<br>that moderate to high CV risk patients with AF and AFL could significantly reduce their<br>risk of CV hospitalisation and mortality with the use of dronedarone in addition to<br>standard baseline therapy compared to baseline therapy alone.<br>The wording of the remit does not adequately reflect the cost burden felt by the NHS<br>as a result of AF and AFL. AF/AFL consumes around 1% of all health care<br>expenditures and indirectly contributes to an additional 2% of the health care costs in<br>the UK (Stewart S et al, 2004). Stroke, a major outcome of AF, is estimated to cost<br>between £2,680 and £4,102 per acute admission (HRG Data - National Tariff<br>2008/2009). 20 to 30% of all acute stroke patients are found to be in AF, this has been<br>the attributed cause of stroke in one-quarter of patients over 80 years of age. (Glader<br>EL et al, 2004. Wolf PA et al, 1987)<br>Costs as a result of hospitalisation (due to cardiac causes) observed in trials involving<br>dronedarone range from £661 (syncope - E31/32) to £4,787 (acute MI - E11/12) with<br>admissions due to arrhythmia or conduction disorder estimated to cost between £898<br>(<70 or w/o cc - E30) and £1,767 (>69 or w cc - E29)(HRG Data, National Tariff<br>2008/2009). By demonstrating a reduction in cardiovascular hospitalisation it is<br>expected that dronedarone could be shown to lead to a reduction in cost burden on<br>the NHS due to these causes.|Comment noted.<br>The background is<br>not an exhaustive<br>summary of the<br>disease. The<br>scope does not<br>take account of the<br>resource<br>consumption or<br>saving associated<br>with a technology<br>though these are<br>important<br>considerations in<br>the economic<br>analysis|
|Timing Issues|Sanofi-aventis|The landmark ATHENA study (presented at Heart Rhythym Symposium, May 2008.<br>Slide set available at http://www.theheart.org/article/867591.do<br> ) showed that<br>dronedarone significantly reduces the risk of CV hospitalisation or mortality in patients<br>with AF/AFL. Dronedarone is the first AAD to have demonstrated such an impact on<br>key clinical endpoints and offers the potential to address an urgent unmet medical<br>need in the provision of treatment within the NHS. To ensure that patients can benefit<br>from this significant advance in treatment we would welcome the opportunity to work<br>with NICE to ensure guidance is available soon after launch.|Comment noted|

## **Comment 2: the draft scope**

|Background<br>information|Sanofi-aventis|The cumulative data from 44 studies in a systematic review of AF (Lafuente-<br>Lafuente et al, 2006) showed that several drugs are effective at preventing<br>recurrences of atrial fibrillation (including flecainide, sotalol and amiodarone)<br>but all of them increased adverse effects and in some studies a trend to<br>increased mortality was observed. The review accepts as a limitation that<br>studies do not typically look at outcomes associated with AF (e.g. stroke) and<br>concludes that it is unclear if the long-term benefits obtained with<br>antiarrhythmic drugs outweigh their risks. It is clear that outcome data and<br>safety profiles are important factors when considering efficacy of anti-<br>arrhythmic drugs.|Comment noted.|
|||||
|The<br>technology/<br>intervention|Sanofi-aventis|Sanofi-aventis believe the description to be accurate.|Comment noted|

|Population|Sanofi-aventis|Sanofi-aventis believe that the appropriate population for dronedarone will be<br>moderate to high CV risk patients with paroxysmal or persistent AF or AFL on<br>top of standard baseline therapy including beta-blockers. This assertion is<br>based on the ATHENA population who were observed to experience the<br>beneficial effects of dronedarone.<br>The expected population will exclude patients with NYHA Class IV heart failure<br>as these patients were excluded from the ATHENA study.|The population has been<br>amended in the scope to take<br>account of the expected<br>clinical use of dronedarone in<br>keeping with the trial<br>population. The population is<br>not limited to moderate and<br>high cardiovascular risk as<br>clinical experts at the<br>workshop believed it would be<br>considered even in people<br>with low risk.<br>Exclusions from the population<br>would depend on the<br>marketing authorisation of the<br>drug|

|Questions for<br>consultation|Sanofi-aventis|•<br>_Would an improved side-effect profile lead to dronedarone replacing other_<br>_AADs used earlier in the treatment pathway?_<br>Sanofi-aventis believe that the positive outcome data seen in ATHENA should<br>represent the main factor when considering the position that dronedarone<br>should adopt in the Atrial Fibrillation / Atrial Flutter management guidelines.<br>•<br>_Are the comparators for AF the same as for flutter?_<br>Patients included in ATHENA, EURIDIS and ADONIS had either AF or AFL so<br>comparators could be considered to be the same. The scope of the CG36<br>guidelines includes "Atrial flutter that is indistinguishable from AF in terms of<br>aim of treatment." That appears to be the same for the trials as mentioned.<br>•<br>_Should non-pharmacological interventions be included as comparators, if_<br>_so which interventions should be included?_<br>Current non-pharmacological therapies are carried out in relatively limited<br>numbers in the UK and are largely performed on patients who have AF that is<br>resistant to pharmacological treatment. This also reflects the NICE<br>recommendations as found in CG36 section 12.3.3 R66. As this group of<br>patients are typically identified much later in the AF management guidelines<br>than the proposed position of dronedarone we believe that a comparison with<br>non-pharmacological therapies would not be appropriate. We are also not<br>aware of any definitive data demonstrating reduction in morbidity and mortality<br>for non-pharmacological treatments compared to standard medical<br>management of AF/AFL.<br>•<br>_Which process would be the most suitable for appraising this technology?_<br>Sanofi-aventis agree that the STA process is the most suitable.|Noted<br>Ablation would be considered<br>early on in the management of<br>AFL and where this is not<br>appropriate it will be managed<br>along the lines of AF.<br>Ablation is not a comparator in<br>the scope as it would be<br>considered before<br>dronedarone and not instead<br>of it.<br>Noted.|
