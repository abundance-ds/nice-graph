||Heart Rhythm UK/<br>Arrhythmia Alliance/<br>British Society for<br>Heart Failure (cont.)|**Background information**<br>**Arrhythmias**<br>Normal heart function requires optimal electrical coordination<br>of contraction. Any abnormal cardiac rhythm, or arrhythmia,<br>which disturbs normal physiological sinus rhythm, reduces<br>cardiac efficiency. Arrhythmias arising in the ventricles,<br>ventricular tachycardia and ventricular fibrillation, can result in<br>insufficient blood being pumped by the heart to sustain life.<br>This is the mechanism of over 80% of sudden cardiac deaths,<br>killing 90,000 people in the UK each year. The most common<br>risk factor for sudden cardiac death is ischaemic heart<br>disease but other causes of reduced cardiac function such as<br>cardiomyopathy also result in increased risk. There are also<br>many inherited conditions which increase the risk of<br>ventricular arrhythmias and sudden death.<br>The most effective way to prevent cardiac arrhythmias is to<br>the treat the underlying heart condition with medications and<br>other interventions. Prevention is an essential part of every<br>patient’s management, but the only effective treatment of life-<br>threatening ventricular arrhythmias is electrical defibrillation.<br>This can be performed with an external defibrillator but<br>unfortunately, because of the short time window before<br>irreversible brain damage, only 5% of people survive a cardiac<br>arrest outside a hospital. The alternative is the implantation of<br>an automatic device, an implantable cardioverter defibrillator<br>(ICD), in people at increased risk of cardiac arrest. This<br>detects and treats any ventricular arrhythmia within a few<br>seconds and is much more effective, successfully defibrillating<br>99% of patients. This technology is recommended in NICE<br>technology appraisal 95 which states that ICD implantation is<br>indicated in primary (no previous cardiac arrest) and<br>secondary (resuscitated cardiac arrest) prevention.|Please see response<br>above.|

||Heart Rhythm UK/<br>Arrhythmia Alliance/<br>British Society for<br>Heart Failure (cont.)|**Implantable cardioverter defibrillators (ICDs)**<br>An ICD is an electronic device implanted in a patient at<br>increased risk of life-threatening arrhythmias. The majority of devices implanted in<br>the UK comprise one or more leads connecting the heart via<br>the central venous system to a device implanted beneath the<br>skin of the chest. A recently<br>developed subcutaneous ICD is able to detect arrhythmias<br>and deliver defibrillation using a lead which is placed under<br>the skin of the chest but does not enter the heart.<br>Subcutaneous ICDs are not directly interchangeable with<br>standard ICDs as they have specific indications and<br>limitations.
