|Background<br>information|Takeda|Lung cancer falls into two main histological categories: non-small-cell lung<br>cancers (NSCLC), which account for 88% of all lung cancers (Royal College<br>of Physicians. National Lung Cancer Audit 2016 (for the audit period of 2015).<br>Published January 2017), and small-cell lung cancers. NSCLC may be<br>grouped by tumour histology into squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma<br>and large-cell carcinoma, with the latter two being collectively referred to as<br>‘non-squamous’ lung cancer.<br>Anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) fusion genes are chromosomal alterations<br>that occur between the tyrosine kinase portion of the ALK gene and other<br>genes. They are believed to be involved in the growth of tumours. ALK<br>translocation can occur in NSCLC of any histology, although it is thought to<br>be most common (almost exclusively) in tumours with adenocarcinoma<br>histology (that is, non-squamous histology) which represent 36% of NSCLC<br>patients (Royal College of Physicians. National Lung Cancer Audit 2016 [for<br>the audit period of 2015]. Published January 2017) and is uncommon in<br>tumours with squamous cell carcinoma histology.<br>People with NSCLC who have an ALK fusion gene are mutually exclusive to<br>epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations, unless later mutation<br>occurs. Accordingly, people with the ALK fusion gene do not receive drugs<br>that inhibit EGFR tyrosine kinase, such as erlotinib, gefitinib and afatinib.|Comment noted. The<br>background section of<br>the scope has been<br>updated accordingly.|

|Economic<br>analysis|Takeda|The economic analysis is specified appropriately.<br>However, the draft scope states that the use of brigatinib is conditional on<br>ALK+ status and the economic modelling should include the costs associated<br>with diagnostic testing for ALK status in people with advanced non-small-cell<br>lung cancer who would not otherwise have been tested, and a sensitivity<br>analysis should be provided without the cost of the diagnostic test.<br>Testing is necessary in order to establish the genetic status prior to first line<br>treatment.  In the ceritinib second line NICE appraisal the Appraisal<br>Committee noted that testing would be done before starting crizotinib so the<br>relevant population would have been tested already and therefore concluded<br>that the costs of testing were not a consideration for the appraisal (NICE<br>Technology appraisal guidance TA295. Ceritinib for previously treated<br>anaplastic lymphoma kinase positive nonsmall- cell lung cancer. Published 22<br>June 2016.). Therefore the economic analysis should not include ALK testing<br>costs.|Comment noted. The<br>scope has been<br>updated accordingly.|
