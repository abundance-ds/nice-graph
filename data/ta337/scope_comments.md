||Norgine<br>Pharmaceutical<br>s Limited|In 1998 the ‘Organisation Mondiale de Gastroentérologie Working Party’<br>introduced a classification for hepatic encephalopathy. The group<br>recommended dividing hepatic encephalopathy into three broad categories: A<br>(acute liver failure), B (porto-systemic bypass without intrinsic liver disease)<br>and C (cirrhosis). The three main hepatic encephalopathy ‘types’ widely<br>referred to within group C are episodic hepatic encephalopathy, persistent<br>hepatic encephalopathy and minimal hepatic encephalopathy.<br>Hepatic encephalopathy can be graded using the Conn score (also called West<br>Haven classification) in which higher scores indicate a higher severity, as<br>follows:<br>Grade 0: No personality or behavioural abnormality detected.<br>Grade 1: lack of awareness, euphoria or anxiety, shortened attention span,<br>impaired performance of addition.<br>Grade 2: lethargy or apathy, minimal disorientation for time or place, subtle<br>personality change, inappropriate behaviour, impaired performance of<br>subtraction.<br>Grade 3: somnolence to semi stupor but responsive to verbal stimuli,<br>confusion, gross disorientation.<br>Grade 4: coma (unresponsive to verbal or noxious stimuli).<br>In 2011, the ISHEN classification system was proposed, but is yet to be fully<br>adopted by the scientific community. It classifies patients as being ‘unimpaired’<br>(no clinical neurophysiological/neuropsychometric changes), having ‘covert’<br>hepatic encephalopathy (patients with minimal hepatic encephalopathy or<br>hepatic encephalopathy with a Conn score of 1 as graded by the West Haven<br>Criteria) or having ‘overt’ hepatic encephalopathy (patients with hepatic<br>encephalopathy with a Conn score of 2,3 or 4 as graded by the West Haven<br>Criteria)|Comment noted - no action<br>required.
