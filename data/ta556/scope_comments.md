|Background<br>information|British Society<br>of<br>Gastroenterolog<br>y|Patients with perianal fistulising disease who fail to respond to medical and<br>surgical therapy will undergo diversion of faecal stream by formation of<br>temporary ileostomy or colostomy. 31%-49% of patients with complex<br>perianal Crohn’s will have permament faecal diversion (Mueller et al J GI<br>surgery 2007;11:529. Galandiuk et al. Ann Surgery 2005;241:796). This is<br>usually followed ultimately by proctectomy, as reanastomosis after temporary<br>diversion usually results in gradual reactivation of active perianal disease. In<br>a recent paper this occurred in 14% of all patients with perianal complex<br>fistulising disease (Williamson et al Dis Colon Rectum 1995;38:389). Stomas<br>remain the greatest fear of patients with inflammatory bowel disease. Patients<br>are often reluctant to have proctectomy as there is no possibility of restoring<br>normal gut continuity after this procedure, and poor healing of the perineal<br>wound after proctectomy is a frequent complication (23% of proctectomies in<br>Yamamoto et al Dis Colon Rectum 1999;42:96)|Comment noted. The<br>background information<br>aims to provide a brief<br>overview of the<br>condition and current<br>practice. No changes to<br>the scope are needed.|
